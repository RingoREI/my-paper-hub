### Beyond accuracy: interoceptive self-appraisal and the meaning of bodily signals
**Citation**:Del Popolo Cristaldi, F., Banellis, L., Bottesi, G., Allen, M.G., 2026. Beyond accuracy: interoceptive self-appraisal and the meaning of bodily signals. Trends in Cognitive Sciences S1364661326002159. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2026.09.010
## 1.Interoception and emotion: a complicated relationship
- **Conventional Perspective**: Using **interoceptive accuracy** to investigate how bodily signals shape emotional experience and mental health.
- A **paradox/mismatch**: The measure that was expected to capture an important aspect of how bodily signals contribute to emotion has produced inconsistent associations with emotional experience and mental health
- This kind of mismatch reveals **a deeper set of methodological and conceptual weaknesses in the way interoceptive signals have been operationalized**
- Conventional ways of utilizing interoceptive accuracy to operationalize interoceptive signals have **overshadowed the processes through which interoceptive information is interpreted and integrated into experience**.
- So the author proposed a new concept called **interoceptive self-appraisal (ISA)**,a higher-order dimension of interoception that captures how individuals interpret and assign adaptive or maladaptive meaning to bodily signals.
- By integrating insights from **appraisal, constructionist, cognitive clinical models and predictive processing accounts**, ISA provides a parsimonious explanation of **how interoceptive information shapes emotion beyond perceptual detection**, with important implications for explaining affective vulnerability across clinical and developmental trajectories.
##  The ‘quiescence trap’ of measuring interoception at rest
- The source of the impasse:**quiescence trap**:the tendency to operationalize interoceptive accuracy through the detection of **a single visceral channel**, most typically the heartbeat, measured at rest.
- Commonly used resting heartbeat detection tasks have limited construct validity, are confounded by noninteroceptive factors such as time estimation and prior beliefs, and show weak or unreliable effects across clinical populations.
- **3 weaknesses of extant paradigms**:
1. Emotions do not typically emerge from quiescent physiological states but rather during perturbations such as stress, threat, effort, or excitement, when multiple physiological systems change over time.
2. A second, related weakness is the widespread practice of measuring ‘interoception’ as a unitary construct, treating performance on a single cardiac task as a proxy for interoceptive accuracy in general.
3. A final weakness concerns the construct validity of interoceptive sensibility, defined as selfreported beliefs about bodily signals.
## interoceptive self appraisal
- The author use the term ISA to refer to the self-relevant evaluative process through which bodily sensations are assigned significance in context.
- Moreover, appraisals can also shape interoceptive signals, highlighting the bidirectional nature of this relationship.
- **Accounts containing elements if ISA**:
1. **Classical appraisal theories**:emphasized the cognitive evaluation of emotionally relevant stimuli as a cause of emotions.
2. **the emotion regulation tradition**:provided a conceptual basis for reappraisal interventions that target bodily arousal.
3. **Constructionist accounts**:argue that emotions emerge when conceptual knowledge is used to make meaning of bodily states, typically by assigning them emotion categories such as ‘fear’ or ‘anger’.
4. **influential cognitive clinical models**:maladaptive interpretations of bodily sensations contribute to emotional distress and represent key targets of psychological intervention.
- **Predictive Processing frameworks**:provide **a candidate mechanism** for formalizing how this interpretative process may be implemented.
1. **At lower levels**, predictive loops **generate and update predictions about bodily states**, supporting interoceptive inference, homeostatic regulation, and the psychophysical processes **typically measured as interoceptive accuracy** (Figure 1A, level 1). 
2. **At higher levels**, inferred emotional states predict bodily trajectories and their expected precision; precision prediction errors signal when bodily evidence is more or less reliable than expected (Figure 1A, level 2).
- The author propose that **ISA operates at this metacognitive level, integrating the content, reliability, and self-relevance of bodily signals as arousal unfolds**. Crucially, such self-inferences also effectively **modulate lower-order precision signals**, acting to amplify the felt intensity of anticipated visceral channels in a spotlight-like manner.
- A common insight:**interoceptive signals are not merely detected but interpreted in light of their implications for the self.**
- However, present perspectives have emphasized different aspects of this process and have not always been brought together within an explicitly interoceptive framework.
- ISA seeks to bring these perspectives into dialog by providing **a streamlined description of a process implicitly embedded across them**. ISA explicitly states that **interoceptive signals are objects of appraisal**, highlighting how individuals evaluate what bodily changes mean for them (e.g., ‘Am I safe?’, ‘Is this threatening?’, and ‘Can I manage this?’).
- The author characterize ISA as a dynamic evaluative process through which bodily signals are assigned self-relevant meaning.
- ISA does not treat visceral afferents as the only bodily inputs to emotion.
- Interoceptive signals provide privileged information about internal physiological changes, while arousal may simultaneously regulate precision weighting across bodily inference and higher-order self appraisal. Yet other bodily signals, including proprioceptive, somatosensory, and exteroceptive cues, such as muscle tension, posture, facial action, sweating, or crying, may also enter ISA when they are appraised as carrying evidence about the self.
- ISA operates **across multiple timescales**, from rapid appraisals of bodily change to more extended, reflective self-evaluations.
- The emotional significance of bodily change can be evaluated automatically, before individuals explicitly identify what they are feeling.
- Arousal regulates precision weighting across both level 1 interoceptive inference and level 2 self-inference, altering how strongly bodily signals and their appraisals influence belief updating.
- The speed and flexibility of this reappraisal, especially during recovery from peak arousal, may help determine whether emotional distress is adaptively downregulated or maladaptively prolonged.
- ISA is inherently **context sensitive**:
1. Similar physiological states may be appraised in markedly different ways depending on the interaction between expected interoceptive precision, metacognitive self-beliefs, and contextual information.
2. Contextual congruence also shapes whether appraisals are adaptive or maladaptive.
3. Adaptive appraisal, therefore, depends on the content and contextual appropriateness of learned beliefs, as well as how confidently and flexibly these beliefs are held. ## Interoceptive self-appraisal: mechanisms and characteristics
- **Adaptive vs. maladaptive ISA**: The adaptive value of interoceptive appraisal depends on whether bodily sensations are interpreted appropriately in context, as well as how flexibly and confidently these beliefs are held. Low interoceptive accuracy can coexist with emotional health, because detection accuracy alone does not determine how bodily changes are interpreted or their emotional consequences.

- **Recursive and bidirectional nature of ISA**:
  - Interoceptive sensations are continuously interpreted, while appraisal can also shape subsequent bodily states.
  - Metacognitive self-inferences may influence autonomic and visceromotor regulation, potentially bringing bodily states closer to those expected under a particular emotional appraisal.
  - For example, interpreting a racing heartbeat as dangerous may amplify anxiety and autonomic arousal, whereas interpreting it as excitement or preparation for exercise may reduce distress.
  - Therefore, ISA is not simply a one-way process in which the brain assigns meaning to incoming bodily signals; appraisal and bodily states may reinforce one another.

- **Organ-specific and context-dependent appraisal**:
  - ISA does not assume a single, global appraisal of the body or a unitary interoceptive trait.
  - Different bodily channels, including cardiac, respiratory, gastrointestinal, and metabolic signals, differ in their dynamics, salience, and contextual relevance.
  - Consequently, the same individual may interpret different bodily sensations in distinct ways: a racing heart as danger, breathlessness as loss of control, gastrointestinal sensations as contamination, or fatigue as incapacity.
  - ISA therefore concerns how channel-specific bodily evidence is weighted and integrated into self-relevant affective meaning in context.

## Clinical implications for affective vulnerability

- **The limitation of accuracy-based accounts**:
  - Accuracy-based accounts often explain psychopathology through impaired detection or noisy interoceptive input.
  - However, individuals with comparable interoceptive accuracy and similar physiological functioning may still experience markedly different emotional outcomes.

- **Maladaptive ISA as a candidate mechanism of affective vulnerability**:
  - Repeatedly interpreting benign or ambiguous bodily sensations as threatening may consolidate rigid higher-order self-beliefs, such as “this sensation means danger” or “my body is unsafe”.
  - These beliefs differ from lower-level predictions about the expected state of a bodily channel: they concern the meaning, reliability, and self-relevance of bodily sensations.
  - Once established, threat-biased self-beliefs may influence future appraisals, increase somatic vigilance, and reinforce emotional distress. Appraisal may also amplify autonomic arousal, further reinforcing the original interpretation.
  - This framework may help integrate findings from alexithymia, anxiety disorders, and panic, by shifting attention from sensory detection alone to the contextual interpretation of bodily evidence.

- **Illustrative example**:
  - An athlete and a patient with panic disorder may detect a similar increase in heart rate with comparable accuracy.
  - The athlete may interpret it as an adaptive preparation for effort, whereas the patient may interpret it as a sign of danger, triggering escalating anxiety.
  - The example illustrates the proposed role of ISA; it is not itself evidence that ISA has already been independently validated as the causal mechanism.

## Developmental trajectories of ISA

- **Early learning and social-environmental scaffolding**:
  - The learning processes that shape interoceptive appraisal begin early in development. Through repeated physiological experiences and social feedback, individuals learn what bodily signals mean, when they are informative, and how much weight to give them in different contexts.
  - Higher-order self-appraisal requires sufficient cognitive, emotional, and neural maturation. The authors therefore identify adolescence as a potentially sensitive period for the development and reorganization of ISA.

- **Why adolescence may be particularly important**:
  - Puberty involves frequent and sometimes ambiguous physiological changes, while social-evaluative demands also increase.
  - At the same time, adolescence is a period when many affective disorders emerge.
  - The authors propose that these changing bodily states and social demands create conditions in which higher-order beliefs about bodily safety, threat, and controllability may consolidate.

- **Temperament and divergent developmental trajectories**:
  - Early traits such as behavioral inhibition (BI) may bias the interpretation of ambiguous internal states.
  - Individuals with higher BI may be more likely to interpret ambiguous bodily sensations negatively and develop intolerance of uncertainty.
  - In a more adaptive trajectory, individuals learn that bodily arousal is transient and manageable, updating their appraisal as physiological states return to baseline.
  - In a more vulnerable trajectory, threat-related appraisal may persist even after peripheral physiology normalizes, reinforcing threat-biased expectations.
  - The authors consequently suggest that emotional resilience may depend partly on learning to interpret bodily fluctuations flexibly across contexts.

## An agenda for future interoception research

- **Central research question**:
  - Future research should investigate how interoceptive accuracy and ISA interact, rather than treating detection accuracy as a sufficient explanation of emotional experience.
  - The key challenge is to measure the interpretation of bodily sensations and determine whether ISA contributes explanatory power beyond existing interoceptive measures.

- **Capture the temporal dynamics of appraisal**:
  - Bodily sensations may acquire emotional significance before, during, and after physiological perturbations.
  - Longitudinal and repeated within-person designs are needed to examine how appraisals emerge, consolidate, and potentially become maladaptive across contexts and development.

- **Study ISA in everyday contexts**:
  - Combining wearable physiological monitoring with ecological momentary assessment (EMA) could reveal how people interpret bodily fluctuations in daily life.
  - These methods may help identify when otherwise benign physiological states become associated with a sense of unsafety, and how individuals differ in their ability to update appraisals across situations.
  - The authors also suggest opportunities for adaptive interventions that target maladaptive appraisals when physiological perturbations occur.

- **Develop methods to measure ISA directly**:
  - **Qualitative methods**: Interpretative phenomenological analysis (IPA) and semi-structured interviews can investigate how individuals turn bodily sensations into personally meaningful interpretations.
  - **Natural language processing (NLP) and large language models (LLMs)**: Language collected through smartphone reports or digital diaries could be analyzed to quantify dimensions of bodily appraisal. These are proposed research tools, not established validated measures of ISA.
  - **Neurocomputational models**: Bayesian and active-inference models could formalize how people update beliefs about bodily states, weight signals according to expected precision, and learn associations between physiological changes and emotional meanings.
  - **Digital body mapping**: Interactive body silhouettes could capture the perceived location, intensity, and emotional valence of bodily sensations, helping researchers investigate organ-specific appraisals.
  - The authors propose integrating these qualitative, computational, and spatial methods in mixed-methods designs.

- **6.4 Establish causal relationships and distinguish bodily channels**:
  - Research should examine whether appraisals differ across cardiac, respiratory, gastrointestinal, and other physiological channels.
  - Pharmacological manipulations, neuromodulation, and other interventions that alter specific brain–body pathways may help distinguish the effects of physiological perturbations from the effects of their interpretation.
  - Better psychophysical measures are also needed to distinguish perceptual bias, precision, and metacognition, and to determine how these processes interact with self-appraisal.

## Conclusion
- The inconsistent relationship between interoceptive accuracy and emotional outcomes may reflect a mismatch between the level commonly measured (signal detection) and the higher-order processes involved in emotional meaning-making.
- ISA makes explicit a shared assumption across appraisal, constructionist, predictive-processing, and clinical accounts: bodily signals matter not only because they are perceived, but also because they are interpreted.
- ISA is a proposed integrative framework rather than an already validated standalone mechanism. Its measurement, boundaries, causal role, and incremental explanatory value remain important questions for future research.
- The central question shifts from “Can the individual accurately detect bodily signals?” to “How does bodily information acquire meaning for the self across changing contexts?”
# **ISA as a hierarchical inferential process**：
- Visceral bodily signals provide bottom-up sensory evidence that supports level 1 interoceptive inferences about the current physiological state (e.g., ‘my heart rate is increased’). 
- These first-order representations are integrated within level 2 metacognitive self-inference, where higher-order self-belief priors shape interpretations of bodily changes (e.g., ‘my body is unsafe’). 
- At both levels, predictions are updated through precision-weighted prediction errors. 
- Level 2 appraisals also modulate the expected precision assigned to lower-level interoceptive information, determining how strongly bodily signals constrain belief updating. 
- Physiological arousal is hypothesized to modulate gain across both levels; emotional expectations predict this dynamic trajectory and elicit increased gain in contextually relevant bodily channels.
# ISA across physiological perturbation and recovery
- The same physiological perturbation can be experienced as anxiety or excitement depending on how bodily changes are interpreted in context. Figure 1B illustrates this principle by showing how similar trajectories of physiological arousal can support markedly different emotional experiences across threat and challenge contexts
- During quiescent conditions (baseline in Figure 1B), interoceptive inputs are often weak, noisy, and assigned a relatively low expected precision, making them insufficient to constrain ISA on their own. The ‘quiescence trap’, therefore, ignores how arousal changes not only the strength of bodily signals but also expectations about how informative those signals should be. Under low-arousal conditions, similar physiological patterns can accompany a wide range of emotional and motivational states, forcing individuals to rely more heavily on contextual cues, beliefs, and prior knowledge when appraising interoceptive sensations.
- As physiological perturbation increases (peak in Figure 1B), interoceptive signals become more salient, behaviorally relevant, and difficult to ignore. We propose that arousal modulates gain at both inferential levels, while inferred emotional states predict its trajectory and the high gain in contextually relevant bodily channels. Self-appraisal integrates these expectations with the evolving content and reliability of bodily evidence. The same increase in arousal may therefore be interpreted as danger in one context and adaptive mobilization in another, generating divergent emotional trajectories despite similar physiological dynamics.
- As physiological activation gradually normalizes (recovery in Figure 1B), interoceptive sensations remain available to ISA, while contextual information accumulates. This creates a critical window in which appraisals can be updated, reinforced, or revised. In threat contexts, residual physiological activation may continue to be interpreted as evidence of vulnerability or loss of control, prolonging distress beyond the perturbation itself. In challenge contexts, the same residual signals may instead be interpreted as evidence of successful coping or adaptive engagement, facilitating emotional recovery.
## My Own Questions and Reflections
**The key limitation of ISA framework is the gap between its theoretical account and its experimental operationalization.(In my own view)**
- Although the authors describe how interoceptive inference and higher-order self-appraisal may interact through predictive processing, they provide relatively limited detail on experimental paradigms that could directly manipulate or distinguish these processes. For example, how could the precision assigned to interoceptive predictions and sensory evidence be experimentally manipulated? How could prediction errors be quantified, and how could lower-level interoceptive inference be dissociated from higher-order metacognitive appraisal?
**Furthermore, compared with existing measures of interoceptive sensibility,namely MAIA, what's the added value of ISA?**
- Several MAIA dimensions already assess how individuals attend to, regulate, trust, and respond to bodily sensations. ISA may offer a more explicitly contextual and self-relevant account of how bodily signals acquire meaning, but it remains unclear how this proposed process can be independently measured and distinguished from existing constructs.
- Maybe the **key challenge** is to establish whether ISA has distinct construct validity, testable mechanistic predictions, and incremental explanatory power beyond existing measures and theories.
